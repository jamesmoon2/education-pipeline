How loops behave

From events to loops

A feedback loop exists when a change travels through a system and eventually influences its starting quantity. Reinforcing loops amplify direction; balancing loops resist a gap or move toward a goal.

Connect it to gardening

More leaf growth can capture more sunlight, enabling still more growth—a reinforcing loop. A thermostat-like watering controller instead compares soil moisture with a target and reduces the gap.

Explanation: Success increases learning, which improves later success: the change amplifies itself.

Map the reinforcing loop in a young garden's plant growth.

  1. Choose the quantity

    Start with plant biomass, something that can rise or fall over time.

  2. Trace the first effect

    More biomass usually creates more leaf area, assuming the plant remains healthy.

  3. Close the loop

    More leaf area captures more sunlight, which supports growth and increases biomass again.

The loop reinforces growth until water, nutrients, space, or another limiting factor becomes important.

Delays change what you see

A delay separates an action from its visible result. Teams may add work when progress looks slow, just as gardeners may water again before moisture reaches deeper roots. Mapping the delay helps prevent overcorrection.

Watch for overcorrection

Before acting twice, ask whether the first action is still moving through the system.

Practice a gardening decision

Pest damage rises as garden beds become denser. What should you inspect first before choosing an intervention?

  • weak: This treats visible damage before checking how density, pest reproduction, and predator populations interact.
  • best: This exposes both reinforcing pest pressure and a potentially delayed balancing response from predators.
  • harmful: A balancing response may be too weak or delayed to prevent serious damage; observation needs a decision threshold.

Debrief: A thoughtful intervention begins by locating the important loops, their relative strength, and the delay before each response becomes visible.

Glossary

Feedback loop

A chain of cause and effect that eventually influences its starting quantity.

Reinforcing loop

A loop that amplifies change in the same direction.

Balancing loop

A loop that counteracts a gap or moves a system toward a goal.

Delay

The elapsed time between a cause and an observable effect in a feedback structure.
